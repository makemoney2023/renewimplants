## Which levels does Ontario define?

The College's authorization page separates minimal sedation (nitrous oxide and oxygen, or a single oral sedative), oral moderate sedation, parenteral moderate sedation, deep sedation, and general anaesthesia. Parenteral routes listed there include intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, submucosal, and intranasal. Deep sedation means the person is not easily aroused and responds to repeated or painful stimulation. General anaesthesia means the person is not arousable even by painful stimulation.<Cite id="rcdso-levels" />

Dentists need current authorization to administer any level, including minimal. Facilities that provide oral moderate sedation, parenteral sedation, deep sedation, or general anaesthesia need a facility permit. A facility that provides only minimal sedation (nitrous oxide-oxygen or oral minimal) does not require a facility permit. A physician who gives sedation or anaesthesia in a dental office must register with the College and stay registered with the College of Physicians and Surgeons of Ontario.<Cite id="rcdso-overview" />

## What does this clinic publish?

Renew's sedation page describes three options in its own words: an oral medication taken before the visit, IV sedation administered by a trained sedation professional, and general anaesthesia for people who want to be completely asleep, with a qualified anaesthesia team. The same page says you will need someone to drive you home.<Cite id="renew-sedation" />

That is the clinic's published description. It is not, by itself, a copy of the facility permit. At the consultation, ask which authorization covers the visit you are booking. The public register is the College's record. This article does not state which permit the clinic holds.

## Why ask which authorization applies?

Authorization is personal to the dentist and, above minimal sedation, to the facility.<Cite id="rcdso-overview" /> A page that lists oral, IV, and general anaesthesia is describing services the clinic offers in its copy. Matching a named drug route to a College level is a question for the consultation, not a guess from a blog.
