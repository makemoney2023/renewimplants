## What did 10 to 18 years of follow-up show?

Maló, de Araújo Nobre, Lopes, Ferro, and Botto reported on 471 patients (286 women and 185 men, mean age 57.7 years), 1,884 implants, and 471 fixed full-arch mandibular prostheses placed with immediate function. Follow-up was 10 to 18 years. Twenty-seven patients had died (5.7%). One hundred forty-nine (31.6%) were lost to follow-up. Cumulative prosthetic survival was 98.8%. Implant cumulative survival was 93% and success was 91.7% up to 18 years.<Cite id="malo-2019" />

Previous biological complications had a hazard ratio of 4.43 for implant failure. Biological complications were 11.8% at implant level. Mechanical complications were 36.7%. For marginal bone loss greater than 3 mm, the odds ratios they reported included smoking 2.72, previous failure of a contiguous implant 3.89, and a biological complication 8.11.<Cite id="malo-2019" />

A systematic review of the All-on-4 concept found mean cumulative implant survival of 94% to 98% between 72 and 132 months, and prosthesis survival of 99% to 100% at 12 months. The authors did not find a significant difference between axial and tilted implants or between maxilla and mandible. The major prosthetic complication they name is fracture of an acrylic prosthesis. They ask for larger randomized trials with long follow-up.<Cite id="gaonkar-2021" />

## Why one clinic's series is not your forecast?

The 93% figure is a cumulative survival estimate from one group's lower-jaw patients, after dropping a large share of people from later visits. It does not tell you how long your bridge will last. It does not include a price, and it is not a personal success rate.

People comparing the idea of All-on-4 with staying in dentures can read [why people choose All-on-4](/blog/why-people-are-choosing-all-on-4). The treatment hub is [All-on-4 dental implants](/services/all-on-4-dental-implants).

## What does maintenance change?

The Maló series linked biological complications with later failure, and linked smoking and prior implant trouble with more bone loss.<Cite id="malo-2019" /> This page will not turn those associations into a rule for your chart. Smoking and diabetes, as separate pooled analyses, are in [dental implants with diabetes or smoking](/blog/dental-implants-with-diabetes-or-smoking).
