<Tldr>**Smoking and diabetes are associated with higher pooled implant-failure rates. They are not a personal yes or no.** A 2015 meta-analysis found failure in 6.35% of implants placed in smokers and 3.18% in non-smokers. A 2022 meta-analysis found higher odds of failure in people with diabetes. Both papers pool other people's studies. Neither one predicts your result.</Tldr>

## What do the pooled smoking numbers say?

Chrcanovic, Albrektsson, and Wennerberg included 107 studies. Implants in smokers failed more often: risk ratio 2.23 (95% CI 1.96 to 2.53). Postoperative infection, in the studies that reported it, had a risk ratio of 2.01 (95% CI 1.09 to 3.72). They also found more marginal bone loss, mean difference 0.32 mm, but that analysis was highly heterogeneous (I² 95%), so this page does not treat 0.32 mm as a stable number. The authors say the results should be interpreted with caution because of uncontrolled confounding.<Cite id="chrcanovic-2015" />

## What did the diabetes review measure?

Al Ansari, Shahwan, and Chrcanovic included 89 publications: 5,510 implants in people with diabetes and 62,780 in people without. The odds ratio for failure was 1.777 (p < 0.001). The authors' conclusion calls that a 77.7% higher risk. Failures were more likely in type 1 than type 2 diabetes (odds ratio 4.477, p = 0.032). The failure difference was statistically significant in the maxilla and not in the mandible. Mean marginal bone loss differed by 0.776 mm (p = 0.027); that bone-loss analysis was also highly heterogeneous, so it is a pooled difference, not a measurement of your jaw.<Cite id="alansari-2022" />

This is not medical advice to change a diabetes medication, and it is not a statement that implants will fail if you have diabetes.

## Why these figures are not a yes or a no?

A meta-analysis averages studies that used different patients, jaws, and follow-up. Your physician manages diabetes. The consultation is where the implant surgeon and the denturist hear the history. Tom Szarski, DD, is the denturist. He does not place implants and he does not treat diabetes.
