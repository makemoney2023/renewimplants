## Why do people struggle with a lower denture?

The McGill consensus statement, from a May 2002 symposium and published by Feine, Carlsson, and colleagues, puts the problem in one sentence. Complete denture wearers are usually able to wear an upper denture without problems, but many struggle to eat with the complete lower denture because it is too mobile. Conventional dentures rely on the residual alveolar ridge and the mucosa for support and retention. Many patients have trouble adapting, especially to the mandibular prosthesis.<Cite id="feine-2002" />

The widespread use of denture adhesive is one indication, they wrote, that these prostheses are inadequate for many wearers. That line is unpacked in [denture adhesive versus implants](/blog/denture-adhesive-vs-implants). The foods they mention are hard or tough ones that wearers report being unable to eat. This page does not add a calorie claim on top of that sentence.<Cite id="feine-2002" />

[Dentures compared with dental implants](/blog/dentures-vs-dental-implants) is the broader comparison. The upper plate, which the statement treats as the manageable one, is in [an upper denture versus upper All-on-4](/blog/upper-denture-vs-all-on-4).

## Is the recommended implant denture the same as All-on-4?

The recommended implant denture is a removable overdenture on two lower implants, and All-on-4 is a fixed bridge on four. Mandibular two-implant overdentures were superior to conventional dentures in randomized and non-randomized trials lasting from six months to nine years. Whatever the attachment (bar, ball, or magnet), people were significantly more satisfied than with new conventional dentures. They found the implant overdenture more stable, rated chewing as easier, and were more comfortable and spoke more easily. Quality of life was significantly higher for patients who received the two-implant overdenture opposing a complete maxillary denture.<Cite id="feine-2002" />

The conclusion follows that evidence: restoration of the edentulous mandible with a conventional denture is no longer the most appropriate first choice, and a two-implant overdenture should become the first choice. Two implants. A denture that is still a denture. [Snap-on dentures versus All-on-4](/blog/snap-on-dentures-vs-all-on-4) is where the fixed four-implant bridge is set beside that clip-in denture.<Cite id="feine-2002" />

## Why does Renew still offer more than one lower option?

The consensus sets a minimum they wanted recognized: two implants and an overdenture, rather than a conventional lower denture alone.<Cite id="feine-2002" /> A fixed lower bridge uses more implants and does not come out. The [lower jaw implants](/services/lower-jaw-implants) page is the clinic's page for that jaw. Which of those prostheses fits depends on the bone, the opposing teeth, and whether the person wants a denture they remove.
