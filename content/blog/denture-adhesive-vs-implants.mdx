## What did the McGill statement say about adhesive?

On 24 and 25 May 2002, a symposium at McGill University produced a consensus led by Feine, Carlsson, and colleagues, published in Gerodontology. Conventional dentures, they wrote, rely on the residual alveolar ridge and the mucosa for support and retention. Many people have trouble adapting, especially to the mandibular prosthesis. The sentence on adhesive is specific: "The widespread use of denture adhesives is one indication that these prostheses are inadequate for many denture wearers."<Cite id="feine-2002" />

They also wrote that numerous denture wearers report they cannot eat many foods, including hard or tough ones, and that diets change in unhealthy ways. That is a population statement. It does not measure one person's tube of adhesive, and this page will not turn it into a nutrition prescription.<Cite id="feine-2002" />

How a full denture compares with implants more broadly is in [dentures compared with dental implants](/blog/dentures-vs-dental-implants).

## Is an implant-held denture the same as All-on-4?

The implant denture in the McGill statement is a removable lower overdenture on two implants. All-on-4 is a fixed bridge on four implants. The trials behind the consensus compared mandibular two-implant overdentures with new conventional dentures, for 6 months to 9 years. Attachment type did not change the direction of the result: bar, ball, or magnet. Participants were significantly more satisfied. They found the overdenture more stable, rated chewing various foods as easier, and were more comfortable and spoke more easily. Quality-of-life ratings were higher for people who received the two-implant overdenture against a conventional upper denture than for people with new conventional dentures.<Cite id="feine-2002" />

The conclusion is just as specific. A conventional denture was no longer the most appropriate first choice for the edentulous mandible. A two-implant overdenture should become the first choice. The upper denture is the one they said people usually manage. The lower denture is the one that moves.<Cite id="feine-2002" />

That overdenture is the family of treatment described in [snap-on dentures versus All-on-4](/blog/snap-on-dentures-vs-all-on-4). It comes out. All-on-4 does not. The lower-jaw version of this evidence is in [a lower denture versus lower implants](/blog/lower-denture-vs-implants).

## Why does Renew separate adhesive, snap-on, and fixed teeth?

Adhesive is something a person adds to a denture that is already made. A snap-on denture is a denture retained by implants. A fixed bridge is screwed or attached so it stays in. The consensus is evidence for the middle option on a lower jaw, not a ranking of all three for every mouth.<Cite id="feine-2002" />

The implant surgeon places the implants. Tom Szarski, DD, is the denturist who designs and fits the teeth and the dentures with the on-site lab. A free consultation includes a 3D scan. The scan is where adhesive, a clip-in denture, and a fixed bridge get separated for one person, instead of being treated as one product.
