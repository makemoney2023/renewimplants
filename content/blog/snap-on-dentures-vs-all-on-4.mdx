## Which one comes out at night?

Renew's denture-alternative page says a snap-on denture, also called an overdenture, clips onto connectors on two or four implants. You can take it out to clean it. During the day it is meant to stay locked.<Cite id="renew-snapon" /> That is the practical difference people feel: it is still a denture.

The same clinic's All-on-4 page describes a full set of upper or lower teeth fixed to four implants in the jaw, and says these teeth stay in, unlike removable dentures.<Cite id="renew-allon4" /> A fixed bridge is cleaned in the mouth. It is not dropped into a cup at night.

The wider comparison, including conventional dentures, is in [dentures versus dental implants](/blog/dentures-vs-dental-implants).

## What does the published All-on-4 concept specify?

Soto-Peñaloza and colleagues describe four implants in the anterior jaw, two of them axial and two angled, supporting a provisional fixed prosthesis that is loaded immediately, with up to 12 teeth. They say the concept was developed for atrophic jaws and to avoid regenerative procedures. They also say the evidence is limited.<Cite id="soto-2017" />

That paper is about a fixed prosthesis. It is not a description of a denture that snaps on and off. If you are weighing the fixed version after years without teeth, the candidacy article is [All-on-4 after years of missing teeth](/blog/all-on-4-dental-implants-after-years-of-missing-teeth).

## Why the fixed option is a different job?

Ontario's Denturism Act defines denturism as the assessment of arches missing some or all teeth, and the design, construction, repair, alteration, ordering, and fitting of removable dentures. Fitting and dispensing in the Act are also about removable dentures.<Cite id="denturism-act" /> A fixed All-on-4 bridge is not that appliance.
