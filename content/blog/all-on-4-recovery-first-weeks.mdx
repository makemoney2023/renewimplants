<Tldr>**There is no single All-on-4 recovery calendar in the studies this page can cite.** In one mandibular series, a provisional fixed acrylic bridge was attached within two hours of placing four implants. That paper does not describe your first weeks of swelling, diet, or time off work. If a mouth that has already been treated does not feel right, call the clinic.</Tldr>

## What did the original study actually time?

Maló, Rangert, and Nobre studied immediate function within two hours using four Brånemark implants to support fixed prostheses in completely edentulous mandibles. Forty-four patients received 176 immediately loaded implants and fixed complete-arch mandibular prostheses in acrylic. Five immediately loaded implants were lost before six months. Cumulative survival was 96.7 percent in the development group and 98.2 percent in the routine group. Prosthesis survival was 100 percent.<Cite id="malo-2003" />

Those rates belong to that retrospective mandibular series. They are not a description of the upper jaw, and they are not a script for the first weeks at home. This page does not copy the study's medication regimen into patient advice.

Same-day teeth, as a separate question, are discussed in [same-day dental implants](/blog/same-day-dental-implants-new-teeth-one-day). The clinic's visit sequence is on [what to expect](/what-to-expect).

## What should you not treat as a schedule?

The papers used here do not establish when swelling peaks, which foods are allowed on which day, or how many days of work you should book off. If a clinic gives you written aftercare, that sheet is the instruction for your case. This article will not invent a substitute.

Long-term complication rates from later follow-up, including mechanical problems years on, are not "the first weeks." How long the implants were still present in a later mandibular series is covered in [how long All-on-4 implants last](/blog/how-long-do-all-on-4-implants-last).

## Why call if it does not feel ordinary?

The 2017 World Workshop separates peri-implant health from disease. Health is the absence of redness, bleeding on probing, swelling, and suppuration. Peri-implantitis adds progressive bone loss, with bleeding or pus, deeper pockets or recession, and bone loss on a radiograph. Diagnosis needs a clinician.<Cite id="berglundh-2018" />

That is not a home test you can finish from soreness alone. If you already have an implant and it does not feel right, use [what to do next](/blog/dental-implant-not-feeling-right) and call [the clinic](/contact-us) at 613-841-6111. The quiz on this page is for people still choosing a treatment, not for someone who needs a clinician today.
