## What does the Denturism Act actually cover?

Section 3 of the Denturism Act, 1991, says the practice of denturism is the assessment of arches missing some or all teeth, and the design, construction, repair, alteration, ordering, and fitting of removable dentures. Section 4, on fitting and dispensing, is also about a removable denture.<Cite id="denturism-act" />

Read that twice before you hear "the denturist does the implants." The statute names a removable appliance. It does not name drilling a fixture into the jaw, and it does not name a bridge that stays screwed in place.

The College of Denturists of Ontario describes the scope in the same frame and includes fabrication of implant-supported dentures among the removable services a denturist is educated to provide.<Cite id="cdo-scope" /> An implant-supported denture that the patient can take out is still a denture. The implants holding it are not the denturist's statutory act.

## Why the fixed teeth are a shared job?

A fixed full arch needs a surgeon to place the implants and a lab to build a prosthesis that does not come out at night. Those are different regulated jobs. Renew's model is that they happen in one building: the implant surgeon, the denturist, and the on-site lab. The denturist does not become the surgeon because the lab is downstairs.
