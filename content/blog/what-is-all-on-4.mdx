## What did the original protocol actually do?

Maló, Rangert, and Nobre published a retrospective series of immediately loaded complete-arch prostheses in edentulous mandibles, or mandibles with hopeless teeth. Inclusion was consecutive among people who accepted the treatment. The acrylic bridge, reinforced by a metal strip, was delivered within two hours. Screw-access holes for the back implants usually sat around the second premolar.<Cite id="malo-2003" />

The first 30 patients were a development group. Twenty-four of them also received 62 rescue implants, in front of and behind the nerve openings. Those extra implants were not part of the provisional bridge. The authors later decided they were not needed. The last third of the patients received only the four immediately loaded implants. When an immediately loaded implant was lost, the provisional bridge survived on the remaining three until a replacement was added.<Cite id="malo-2003" />

Life tables show four immediately loaded implants lost before six months in the development group and one in the routine group. Cumulative implant survival was 96.7% and 98.2%. Prosthesis survival was 100%.<Cite id="malo-2003" />

## Is All-on-4 the same thing as any full arch?

A full arch means a bridge that replaces a whole row. All-on-4 is one way to support that row: four implants, with the back ones tilted, loaded early. [How four implants compared with six in later reviews](/blog/all-on-4-vs-all-on-6) is a separate question, and the reviews do not treat the count as interchangeable for every jaw. [Full arch treatment versus individual implants](/blog/full-arch-vs-individual-dental-implants) is a different comparison again. People deciding whether the idea fits a life in dentures can read [why people look at All-on-4](/blog/why-people-are-choosing-all-on-4).

The 2003 paper is a lower jaw. It is one clinic. It is not a personal success rate, and it is not a price.

## Why does Renew describe it as one technique?

The [All-on-4 dental implants](/services/all-on-4-dental-implants) page describes a full set of teeth fixed to four implants, planned from a 3D scan at a free consultation. The same site says some upper jaws are planned with six or more implants. That is a clinical choice after the scan, which is why this page keeps the 2003 definition tight.<Cite id="renew-all-on-4" />

The implant surgeon places the implants. Tom Szarski, DD, is the denturist who designs and fits the teeth with the on-site lab. The paper's acrylic bridge, delivered within two hours, is the historical protocol. How this clinic stages temporary teeth and a later bridge is a separate article from the definition.
