<Tldr>**All-on-4 vs all-on-6 compares how many implants hold a fixed full-arch bridge.** A maxillary review found no significant survival difference, and significantly more bone loss with four implants. A 2026 pool of the named concepts also showed high survival for both, with wide heterogeneity. Neither paper chooses the number for your jaw.</Tldr>

## What did four-versus-six reviews actually find?

Sharaf, Wang, Mashrah, Xu, Haider, and He pooled 53 studies of maxillary fixed complete dentures: 20,251 implants in 4,713 arches, mean follow-up 3.8 years. Mean implant survival was 98.5% (95% CI 97.8 to 99.1) with four implants and 97% (95% CI 95.2 to 98.4) with six. That survival difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.06). Prosthesis survival was 99.8% versus 99.6% (p = 0.37). Mean marginal bone loss was 1.02 mm (95% CI 0.79 to 1.26) with four implants and 0.46 mm (95% CI 0.20 to 0.72) with six (p < 0.01).<Cite id="sharaf-2024" />

The abstract reports no significant difference in technical or mechanical complications, or in biological complications. The technical rates they calculated were 20.7 per 100 prostheses with four implants and 17.9 with six (p = 0.74). Biological rates were 2.0 and 0.8 (p = 0.20). A later conclusion sentence says six implants can decrease complications. That sentence is stronger than the tests. This page uses the tests.<Cite id="sharaf-2024" />

Shao and colleagues pooled 55 studies of the named All-on-4 and All-on-6 concepts. At one year, implant survival was 99.20% versus 100%. From one to five years it was 99.66% versus 98.55%. At five years or more it was 98.14% versus 97.50%. Short-term bone loss was 0.77 mm versus 0.85 mm. At five years it was 1.28 mm versus 0.94 mm. The authors report high long-term survival for both techniques and similar biological complications between them. They also note high heterogeneity, and only one randomized trial compared the two concepts directly.<Cite id="shao-2026" />

## Why is a pooled percentage not your implant count?

These are different papers. Sharaf is maxillary fixed dentures, and nearly every four-implant study in it used two straight implants and two tilted ones. Shao names All-on-4 and All-on-6 and still warns that most included studies were lower-level evidence. Putting 98.5% from one paper next to 98.14% from the other, as if they were one experiment, would invent a comparison neither group ran.<Cite id="sharaf-2024" /><Cite id="shao-2026" />

A longer mandibular series, with its own lost-to-follow-up problem, is in [how long All-on-4 implants lasted in that follow-up](/blog/how-long-do-all-on-4-implants-last). The original four-implant protocol is in [what All-on-4 meant in the 2003 series](/blog/what-is-all-on-4). Replacing teeth one by one is a different comparison, covered in [full arch treatment versus individual implants](/blog/full-arch-vs-individual-dental-implants).
