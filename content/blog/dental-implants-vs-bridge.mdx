## What does a bridge ask of the neighbouring teeth?

Hebel, Gajjar, and Hofstede published a peer-reviewed comparison in the Journal of the Canadian Dental Association in 2000, volume 66, pages 435 to 438. The options they discuss for a single tooth are a fixed partial denture, a resin-bonded restoration, and a single-tooth implant. They wrote that a conventional three-unit bridge is familiar work for most restorative dentists, and that it requires reduction of the abutment teeth. That reduction, they wrote, results in an increased incidence of endodontic therapy and root decay.<Cite id="hebel-2000" />

They drew a line at the condition of those teeth. Large existing restorations might make abutment preparation useful. Virgin teeth, or teeth with small restorations, would be damaged and placed at higher risk. They also wrote that cement loss or washout under a retainer can lead to tooth loss, and that a bridge is one restoration: if one part fails, the whole restoration fails, often with loss of an abutment tooth. The implant's advantage, in their words, is that the adjacent teeth stay in their current state of health and are not splinted into a larger restoration.<Cite id="hebel-2000" />

The paper says patients should hear both sides before choosing. It does not rank the options for a person who is losing many teeth at once.

## When is a full arch a different comparison?

A three-unit bridge borrows strength from two teeth to fill one space. When those neighbours are themselves failing, the 2000 article's premise (sound or restorable abutments beside a single gap) no longer describes the mouth. ## Why is a three-unit bridge a different job from a full jaw?

The journal article is explicit about its subject: replacement of a single tooth. Using it to argue for or against All-on-4 would stretch a three-unit bridge into a jaw it never measured.<Cite id="hebel-2000" /> The useful part, for someone with one or two failing teeth next to sound ones, is the preparation of those neighbours. The useful part, for someone whose remaining teeth are the problem, is noticing that the article does not cover that mouth.
